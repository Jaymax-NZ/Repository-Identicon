Repository-Identicon — the production system

Every top-level routine in repository-identicon.py and text-identicon.py, one page per module. Each item gives its signature, what it hands back, and one sentence saying what it is for. What its terms mean is the glossary's job — hover any item for the entries it uses, or read Terms straight through.

Items are numbered page.item so they can be named in a sentence. A notched connector is an off-page reference: click it to jump to the item it names. An arrow points the way the work goes. Out of scope and drawn nowhere: work-in-progress/, tests/ and reference/.

·Terms

the words this repository uses in a particular way. No item on a page restates one of these; it points at them instead.

termwhat it means herewhere
Identity
keyThe one string the mark depends on: <mapping version>:<seed>, hashed verbatim.1.16 · 1.14
seedThe identity alone, without the version: github.com/owner/repo.1.5 · 1.12
mapping versionThe integer stamped into the key saying which colour rule drew it. Currently 3.1.15 · 2.8
sourceWhere the seed was arrived at: explicit, override, remote, toplevel or path — or key and remap, which outrank all of them.1.5
overrideA committed .repository-identicon holding a seed, outranking the git remote.1.6 · 1.7
recorded keyThe payload line of the key file, which outranks every derivation.1.2 · 1.4
seed driftWhat this repository would seed as today, differing from what is recorded. Reported, never acted on.6.1
maskingAn override in force while the git remote says something else.6.1
reseed / remapThe two deliberate acts that move a mark: reseed adopts today's seed, remap keeps the seed and moves the mapping.6.1
The mark
the markThe grid and the colour together — what a reader recognises.2.11 · 2.12
gridThe 5×5 of booleans, from digest characters 0–14, drawn down the centre column and mirrored outwards.2.2
hueA fraction of a turn, from the last seven digest characters.2.3
the warpThe raised-cosine reweighting that makes the hue advance faster through the blue-green arc.2.4
lightness / chromaOklab. Lightness is held at 0.60 for every hue; chroma is capped at 0.26, not flattened.2.8
gamut chromaThe most chroma sRGB will take at that hue and lightness.2.5
Geometry
blockThe pixel edge of one grid cell — the specified quantity.3.5
borderThe margin in pixels on each side. Chrome rather than content.3.5
canvas5·block + 2·border. Always derived, never specified.3.5
fittingChoosing the largest block that fits a canvas someone else fixed.3.6
Names
short idThe first 12 characters of sha256(key).3.1
discriminatorThe first 6, for telling apart two projects sharing a basename.3.1
project nameThe last /-separated segment of the key, or all of it.3.2
badge labelOne or two upper-case characters, for a medium that can show no more.3.3
icon theme name<tool prefix>-<short id>. A name resolved through the icon theme, never a path.3.4
In the repository
artifactOne of the seven rendered files in .identicon/ — a cache with a canonical location, not a source.6.3 · 6.10
the key fileThe exception: the only file in .identicon/ that is a source of truth.1.4
priorstem.prior.suffix — the bytes a run replaced, kept one level deep.6.6
currentTrue when every artifact already holds the bytes it should.6.1
Renderings
renderingBytes for one medium, derived from the key. Not part of conformance.4.1
styleWhich rendering emit asks for: icon, image, text, full, banner or line.4.1
protocolHow an image reaches the terminal: iterm2, kitty, or text meaning it cannot.4.3 · 4.5
depthHow much colour the terminal takes: truecolor, the 256-cube, or none.4.2
The text mark
octantOne of 256 patterns over a 2×4 lattice; one glyph covers four grid cells.5.3 · 5.2
the tripleThree emoji squares standing for the colour, where neither an image nor an escape sequence survives.5.4 · 5.6
arrangementThe order the three squares are laid out in, taken from the grid and never from the colour.5.9 · 5.10
the paletteThe nine Unicode colour squares, anchored on the colour word in the character's name.5.11
Conformance
vectorOne pinned (key, md5, grid, foreground) in vectors.json.9.3 · 9.2
portAnother implementation of the specification, in any language.9.4 · 9.5
conformingReproduces the grid and the colour of every vector.9.6
vendoringCarrying your own copy of the derivation rather than depending on this one.9.5
